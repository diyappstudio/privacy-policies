“Personal data” is information linked or reasonably linkable to an identified or identifiable individual. It excludes de-identified or publicly available information and material outside the law's scope, including HIPAA, GBPA, non-profit entities, higher education, employment data, FCRA, the Driver's Privacy Protection Act of 1994, the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, and the Farm Credit Act.

Section I.C explains the categories of personal data processed and their purposes. Section I.F describes shared-data categories and the categories of third-party recipients. Section I.K covers data sales and targeted advertising.

The Colorado-residents table in Section II.B describes your CDPA rights and explains how to exercise them.

1. How to submit A request under CDPA?

We respond within 45 days after receipt. If reasonably necessary because of the complexity or number of requests, we may extend this once by another 45 days. We will tell you within the initial 45 days and give the reason.

If we decline to act, we will notify you without undue delay and within 45 days of receipt, explain the refusal and give appeal instructions. Within 60 days of receiving an appeal, we will provide a written explanation of action taken or not taken and the reasons. If an appeal is denied, you may complain to the Connecticut Attorney General at https://www.dir.ct.gov/ag/complaint/ or (860) 808-5318.

We provide responses free of charge up to twice annually, except for manifestly unfounded, excessive or repetitive requests. If commercially reasonable efforts cannot authenticate you and your request, we may ask for additional information reasonably needed for authentication. If authentication remains unsuccessful, we cannot grant the request.

The Utah Consumer Privacy Act (“UCPA”) provides the rights below to Utah residents acting in an individual or household setting, rather than a commercial or employment setting. “Personal Data” means information linked or reasonably linkable to an identifiable person, excluding de-identified and publicly available data.

Section I.C describes processed personal-data categories and purposes. Section I.F identifies the categories of personal data shared and third-party recipients. Section I.K discusses sales and targeted advertising.

F. Notice to nevada residents

Nevada residents may opt out of sales of certain personal information. Subject to exceptions, Nevada law treats a “sale” as exchanging specified types of personal information with another person for monetary consideration. We do not currently sell personal information under that definition. Nevertheless, Nevada residents may submit verified sales opt-out requests. We will record those instructions and apply them if our policy changes. Send requests to support.metaldetector@diyappsstudio.com.
